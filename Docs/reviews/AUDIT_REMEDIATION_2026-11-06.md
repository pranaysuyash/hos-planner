# HOS audit remediation, 2026-11-06

## Scope and decisions

This work improves the trust boundary of the local scenario planner. It does not establish actual hours-of-service status, an ELD record, driver authorization, or compliance with an applicable exemption.

- Validation rejects missing, invalid, overlapping and materially gapped duty intervals as indeterminate, retaining unknown totals. The accepted floating-point continuity tolerance is also used by the break accumulator. Team aggregation preserves indeterminate assessments and any separate modeled findings without interpreting unknown as an actual violation.
- The itinerary carries `assessment` and reasons along with its legacy `isLegal` flag. Indeterminate connected reviews cannot be published as a duty result. A modeled result is still a scenario bounded by its assumptions, not a legal or operational determination.
- The local `HOS_SCHEDULE_UPDATED` event carries `schemaVersion: 1`, `source: scenario_model`, typed `assessment` (`modeled_feasible`, `modeled_violations`, or `indeterminate`), assessment reasons, and actual sender metadata. The legacy `isLegal` flag remains for compatibility and is false for indeterminate scenarios. Consumers must inspect the typed fields and must not treat this unbound broadcast as a reviewed shipment or operational authority. Older messages without these fields remain unverified.
- The scenario timeline no longer fills unmodeled time as off duty or connects separated intervals as if recorded. Its printable summary retains the timeline and modeled totals but no longer fabricates a carrier, equipment, mileage, prior-cycle history, driver certification, or signed date. This is a planning artifact, not an official roadside log.
- The audit report checks the complete itinerary as well as the currently selected driver's audit when describing uncertainty. Existing UI screenshots and browser behavior are not proved by static rendering.

## Evidence and remaining work

Before remediation, targeted tests reproduced incorrect break handling for a difference below the validation tolerance, absent assessment aggregation, and official/certified print claims. Product test configuration now includes both `.test.ts` and `.test.tsx`, so the printable-view regression runs. After remediation `npm test` passed 10 files and 62 tests, `npm run typecheck` passed, and `git diff --check` passed. These are local T2/S2 checks; the printable check uses server-rendered markup, not a browser or printer.

The rule engine still models a fixed 192-hour lookback instead of a carrier's calendar-day cycle, assumes an initial scenario rather than complete preceding driver history, and does not validate split-sleeper pairings. Its 70-hour findings and remedy need domain review: the model currently considers non-driving on-duty intervals and assumes a restart policy. The [FMCSA HOS summary](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations) and [interstate property-carrier guide](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/2022-04/FMCSA-HOS-395-DRIVERS-GUIDE-TO-HOS%282022-04-28%29_0.pdf) describe conditions including 60/7 versus 70/8, carrier-designated day boundaries and optional qualifying restarts. These sources do not validate the implementation or cover every exception.

The full end-to-end event consumer, legacy events and saved records, mobile layout, keyboard/print behavior, and a real driver's history require separate validation. Do not infer that the scenario is safe to dispatch from passing tests.

## Follow-up: cycle history and connected publication

The earlier cycle paragraph above describes the pre-follow-up engine. The model now reports the cycle as `indeterminate` for every current input because it has no carrier-selected 60/7 or 70/8 rule, carrier-defined day boundary, complete preceding duty history, or recorded restart election. A valid interval sequence still receives full-rest shift-model findings and totals; `shiftChecksPassed` is a scenario result, not a legal assessment. Malformed histories retain unknown totals and a null shift result. The legacy `isCompliant`/`isLegal` booleans fail closed when the overall assessment is unknown. The separate `cycleIllustration` preserves the prior 192-hour arithmetic and its assumed 34-hour restart explicitly as an illustration, not a calculation of actual availability or a 70-hour violation. It does not add a cycle violation to the report or prescribe a mandatory restart.

[FMCSA's HOS summary](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/regulations/hours-service/summary-hours-service-regulations) states the 60/70-hour limit as a restriction on driving and describes a 34-hour restart as optional. The [FMCSA property-carrier driver guide](https://www.fmcsa.dot.gov/sites/fmcsa.dot.gov/files/2022-04/FMCSA-HOS-395-DRIVERS-GUIDE-TO-HOS%282022-04-28%29_0.pdf) describes the carrier-designated 24-hour boundary and the rolling days. These sources were inspected on 2026-11-06; neither validates the scenario engine or all exceptions.

**Compatibility consequence:** the current shared shipment stage requires a positive `details.isLegal` result but has no place for the missing cycle inputs. To avoid publishing an invented positive or negative decision, `resultForDutyPlan` rejects an indeterminate result with its missing-cycle reason. Thus the connected HOS stage cannot currently publish a new positive result; local scenario planning and the read path for previously saved supported inputs are retained. Recovery needs a versioned shared contract, validated carrier rule and day boundary, history and restart provenance, explicit unknown states, independent domain review, and end-to-end tests. Existing records are not retroactively certified.

Before this follow-up all five new boundary tests failed, including a false cycle finding for on-duty non-driving work. Afterward focused tests cover indeterminate cycle state, preservation of shift findings and illustrated arithmetic, optional restart treatment, team reason aggregation, event payloads and publication refusal. Updated older tests preserve the shift and saved-input checks while no longer treating them as legal certification. Product test and typecheck outcomes are reported to the shared audit owner. Browser/printing, carrier data, legal interpretation and real ELD behavior remain unverified.

## Follow-up: save an indeterminate connected review

This section supersedes the publication-refusal detail above. The native adapter can now save a version 1 indeterminate duty review with `ok: false`, `isLegal: false`, the exact reviewed inputs, itinerary, audit and reasons. The service validates the review version, current driver and declared rest, requires a nonempty explanation, and stores the review and event without claiming a positive or negative legal finding. The Product 03 action says “Save modeled duty review”; its status explains that dispatch remains blocked. The shared workspace, canvas and inspector distinguish `indeterminate` from `rejected`.

The local service does not implement validated cycle-history provenance. It rejects **all** newly submitted positive HOS results and independently blocks dispatch for any retained positive HOS result, regardless of client-supplied verification flags. Legacy records remain readable, are shown as `unverified`, and are not rewritten. Shipment input changes make the saved review stale. A previously dispatched scenario remains readable; this change does not reverse historical state. A future authoritative result needs a versioned rule/history contract and server-side validation, not a client boolean.

A Product 03 integration test uses the real adapter and canonical shipment domain to check retained native evidence, event payload, indeterminate status, blocked dispatch and staleness after amendment. Domain tests additionally exercise wrong driver/version/missing reasons, forged flags and legacy positive records. A local HTTP-level test checks persisted review, rejection of a forged positive update and dispatch, and verifies both rejected operations leave the stored bytes unchanged. The affected generated clients were synchronized only after their root and nested Git status were clean and their nine copies matched; Product 09 remains registry-only. These are local T2/S2 contract tests, not a browser, real ELD, legal-domain or physical dispatch validation.

## Options to restore a positive modeled duty workflow

The present service always adds an HOS blocker for a current positive or indeterminate result: `dispatchBlockers` cannot establish cycle eligibility and `validateResult` refuses newly submitted positive HOS results. HOS is therefore an unconditional blocker, **not necessarily the only blocker**. The other six stages must be current and accepted, carrier evidence must have a valid future expiry, and all shipment exceptions must be resolved. An isolated synthetic probe found that the service formerly failed to flag missing or malformed carrier expiry even though its UI reported unknown freshness. The server was corrected to distinguish missing, invalid, expired and current expiry, with an adversarial regression; an HOS fix alone must not skip that or other prerequisites.

Three paths remain:

1. **Current containment:** save and inspect an indeterminate modeled review. This preserves useful planning evidence and retains all blockers, but does not restore new scenario dispatch.
2. **Bounded synthetic-history model:** add explicit per-driver scenario history and independently calculate supported shift and cycle outcomes on the service. A successful result could satisfy only the **local modeled scenario** HOS stage after the schema, rule engine and tests below are implemented. It would not establish actual ELD records, legal compliance or operational authorization. This is the candidate technical direction; no carrier data or rule selection is silently inferred.
3. **Actual-record integration:** ingest authenticated driver/carrier evidence with provenance, permissions, freshness, corrections and qualified review. This needs a separate provider, privacy, operating and release decision; it must not be inferred from the local scenario capability.

Minimum dependencies for path 2:

- **Versioned data contract and ownership.** Add a scenario duty-history object bound to the shipment driver and input revision. It needs a declared 60/7 or 70/8 cycle and operating scope; a carrier-designated 24-hour day start and home-terminal time zone; the scenario departure time; ordered, contiguous, timestamped status intervals covering the required preceding cycle and initial shift/break context; and explicit, qualifying restart-election provenance if a restart is used. Preserve unknown values as unknown. Treat data as operator-declared synthetic scenario input, with source and schema version. Confirm the exact coverage, inclusivity and correction semantics against a qualified domain reviewer before fixing the schema.
- **Time and chronology.** Define time-zone, daylight-saving transition, carrier-day boundary, interval-crossing and rounding behavior explicitly. Include pre-departure driving, loading, other carrier work and paid non-carrier work in the supplied history when applicable. The existing `priorRestHours` and route mileage alone cannot establish those facts. Reject gaps, overlaps, nonfinite values, impossible ordering, unsupported statuses and incomplete time rather than assuming rest.
- **One canonical rule implementation.** Reuse a shared, versioned, deterministic rule engine from P03 and the local service so the server can recompute the exact reviewed scenario instead of trusting `ok`, `isLegal`, `cycleVerified` or another client claim. Account for full interval overlap with carrier days, on-duty and driving time, 60/7 and 70/8 selection, optional qualifying restart and the driving-specific cycle restriction. Keep split-sleeper, adverse conditions, short-haul, passenger, cross-border and other exceptions explicitly out of scope until individually implemented and reviewed. Shift checks must use a supported declared initial state; a synthetic fresh start is not real history.
- **Contract and migration.** Introduce an explicit versioned modeled outcome and source/revision fingerprint, distinct from authenticated operational authority. The service must validate and recompute it under the same serialization and optimistic-revision rules as other stages; changed driver, route, rest, cycle settings or history must make the result stale. Preserve old positive and incomplete records as unverified; never backfill history or reclassify them automatically. Review the identity and privacy implications of adding history to event payloads before real data is permitted.
- **Independent evidence and UX.** Build independently derived, dated property-carrier examples for both cycles, day rollover, midnight/time-zone transitions, multi-day intervals, exactly-at-limit and limit-crossing driving, on-duty-but-not-driving, optional restart, malformed history, stale revisions and forged client fields. Test producer → shared contract → server → workspace and browser save/reload, as well as all other dispatch blockers. Obtain qualified HOS review before stronger claims. The existing 192-hour illustration and its test fixtures are not an oracle.
